## Words you will hear (glossary)

- **FACT + the 3 T's**: memory aid for the algorithm: Frames → clock, All claims, Check (Trade, Timing, Tenure), Tell the verdict. Hook: place it in time, list what it claims, then ask - did it trade, was it there, is it old enough?
- **Market record (tape)**: public 1-minute data for NSE: lowest and highest price (or index level) in each minute, and shares traded for stocks. We use Yahoo Finance's copy - a data vendor, not NSE's own trade-by-trade record. It has no names.
- **Index level**: an index such as the Nifty 50 is a calculated number, not something bought and sold. We check that a stated level was within the recorded range - not that a trade happened.
- **Knowability (price-timing consistency)**: was the stated price inside the market's recorded range just before it was said? It does NOT show what the speaker saw or knew.
- **Claim**: any price, quantity or trade mentioned in the video, e.g. 'Nifty is at 22,700'.
- **Encoder**: a part of the software that 'reads' one kind of input: the chart, the text on screen, or the speech.
- **OCR**: software that reads text from a picture. ASR: software that turns speech into text.
- **Ratchet / day-high**: the chart's 'highest price so far today'. It can only go up, like a high-water mark, so it tells us the time.
- **τ (tau) / time map**: how many seconds the video is behind or ahead of the real market clock.
- **PASS / FAIL / ABSTAIN**: fits the records / impossible under every sensible reading / not enough evidence to tell.
- **Assumption**: something we take as given, written down openly (e.g. 'the trade was on NSE').
- **Baseline**: a simpler method we compare against - here mostly our own first version.
- **Ablation**: switching one part off to prove it is needed.
- **Held-out / in-sample**: tested on data never seen while building / tested on the same data used to fix it (weaker).
- **Certificate**: the downloadable proof sheet: answers, evidence, assumptions and data fingerprints (hashes).

## Slide 2: A wrong number on air looks exactly like a right one

Two real cases. First, our own CNBC-TV18 recording: 'Bank Nifty now at 55,150' - the recorded minute was 55,114 to 55,157, so it fits. Second, NDTV Profit's YouTube captions: 'Nifty50 24,616.6' - the record says 22,616.6. One digit differs, so it is most likely a caption error or a slip of the tongue; either way the number viewers read is impossible. And automatic checking is risky: our own first version wrongly called 36 of 37 statements impossible.

**If the panel asks:**
- **Q:** So did the anchor make a mistake?
  **A:** We cannot say. The caption text is impossible; who caused it the record cannot show.

## Slide 3: The task: for every claim, is it possible, impossible, or can't we tell?

Given a video, the market's minute record and the date the video was published, we give every claim three answers - Trade, Timing, Tenure - with evidence. The key rule: say FAIL only when the claim is impossible however the sentence is reasonably read, and only when all four conditions on the right hold. Otherwise ABSTAIN. Note the app's names for the three checks, shown in brackets.

**If the panel asks:**
- **Q:** Who decides what a 'sensible reading' is?
  **A:** The software tries the readings a listener could reasonably have: which stock, whether the number is a price now or a target, and how it was rounded. All must fail for FAIL.

## Slide 6: How it works: three readers build a list of claims; three checks judge it

Pictures go to two readers: the frame reader reads the chart and works out the market time; the text reader reads prices, quantities and profit figures. Sound goes to the audio reader. Everything becomes one claim list with market times. The three checks run independently against the market record, and their answers form the certificate.

**If the panel asks:**
- **Q:** What happens without a chart on screen?
  **A:** The frame reader says so, and claims are timed with the recording computer's clock, labelled.

## Slide 9: Worked example: one real claim from pixels to verdict

One real sentence end to end. The anchor says '22,700 is where the index finds itself'. The frame reader reads the boxed numbers; of the 7 trading days in our data only 6 October fits, at 13:02:55 give or take 4 seconds. The speaker may be reading a screen up to 3 minutes old, so we check 12:59:51 to 13:02:59. The 13:02 minute ranged 22,693 to 22,706; allowing for rounding, 22,700 fits: TIMING PASS. No share count was said, so TRADE can't tell; TENURE can't tell because the rule depends on who the speaker is.

**If the panel asks:**
